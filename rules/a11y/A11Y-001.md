## Context & Problem
AI web generation routines frequently output light gray text (such as `#9CA3AF` or `#CBD5E1` on pure `#FFFFFF` backgrounds) in an effort to emulate minimalist design aesthetics. On white surfaces, light gray text achieves a contrast ratio of barely 2.1:1, drastically failing the WCAG 2.1 AA threshold of 4.5:1 for standard body copy. Low contrast creates severe barriers for people with low vision, visual fatigue, age-related vision loss, or anyone viewing a mobile screen under outdoor sunlight. Active UI component boundaries (input borders, active tab outlines) similarly require at least 3:1 contrast against adjacent background colors.

## Detection Logic
Automated headless browser audit using `axe-core` executes against the fully rendered DOM tree. It inspects every rendered text node, evaluating the computed `color` against the effective background color of parent or underlying stacked elements. Any standard text node with a contrast ratio below 4.5:1 (or large text >= 18pt / 14pt bold with contrast below 3:1) generates a violation. Input borders and interactive outlines are checked against the 3:1 non-text contrast threshold.

## Remediation Steps
1. Upgrade faint text utility classes: replace `text-gray-400` with high-contrast neutral tokens such as `text-stone-700` (#3f3f46 / 7.5:1) or `text-zinc-800` (#27272a / 12.6:1) on light surfaces.
2. For dark mode color schemes, calibrate surfaces to maintain at least 4.5:1 contrast: pair dark charcoal backgrounds (`#121212`) with soft white or high-contrast silver typography (`#F4F4F5` / 15.8:1), never muddy dark grays.
3. Form input borders must maintain at least 3:1 contrast against the surrounding form background (e.g., use `#71717A` instead of faint `#E4E4E7`).
4. Avoid placing body text over variable background images or gradients without a solid or high-opacity backdrop overlay (`bg-stone-900/80 backdrop-blur-sm`).
